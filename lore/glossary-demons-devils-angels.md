# Glossary: Demons, Devils and Angels

A working reference for *Pestilence*, drawn from real-world scripture, apocrypha, demonology and
esoteric tradition (see CLAUDE.md, §2). In this campaign "demon" and "devil" are used
interchangeably, in the medieval way; rank and domain matter more than family.

**How to use it:** pick a fiend whose domain fits the scene's sin or theme, then look for its
**opposing saint or angel** and **traditional weaknesses**: those are ready-made *lore
weaknesses* (CLAUDE.md, §4). Entries marked 🜏 have strong plague or France hooks.

**Anachronism note:** most demonologies postdate 1348 (Binsfeld 1589, Michaëlis 1612, the
*Lesser Key* 17th c., de Plancy 1818). We're mythologizing, so they're fair game; treat them as
"what the learned will one day write down", or as truths the characters discover first.

**Accuracy note:** grimoires disagree between editions and translations (legion counts, ranks,
spellings). Details here follow the most common readings; check a primary source before a
detail becomes canon in a journal.

## The infernal court (Dictionnaire Infernal) 🜏

De Plancy's Hell is a parody of a royal court, with titles, ministers and chivalric orders.
It's ideal for scheming, political devils in a France of kings and bishops.

- **Beelzebub**: supreme chief of the empire of Hell; founder of the **Order of the Fly**
  (*l'Ordre de la Mouche*). 🜏 *Flies, rot, the plague as his court's livery.*
- **Satan**: leader of the opposition.
- **Eurynome**: **Prince of Death**, Grand Cross of the Order of the Fly. Long teeth, a body
  covered in sores, a fox-skin cloak; **feeds on corpses**. 🜏 *The plague's natural patron.*
- **Moloch**: Prince of the Land of Tears.
- **Pluto**: Prince of Fire.
- **Pan**: Prince of the Incubi. **Lilith**: Princess of the Succubi.
- **Leonard**: Grand Master of the Sabbath; presides over witches' gatherings as a black goat.
- **Baalberith**: Chief Secretary and Keeper of the Archives; Master of Alliances (pacts). 🜏
  *Every bargain made in this campaign is written down by him.*
- **Proserpine**: Archduchess, sovereign of mischievous spirits.
- **Adramelech**: Grand Chancellor, Grand Cross of the Order of the Fly.
- **Astaroth**: Grand Treasurer.
- **Nergal**: Chief of the secret police.
- **Baal**: Commander-in-chief of the infernal armies.
- **Leviathan**: Grand Admiral.
- **Ukobach**: stoker of Hell's furnaces; credited with inventing fried food and fireworks.
- **Verdelet**: Master of Ceremonies; carries witches to the sabbath.
- **Nybbas**: herald of dreams and visions (a lesser, mocked servant). *Good for the Archbishop's
  dream.*

**Ambassadors to the nations** 🜏

| Ambassador | Posting |
|---|---|
| **Belphegor** | **France** |
| **Mammon** | **England** (and English Gascony, Bordeaux included) |
| Belial | Turkey |

*Belphegor as Hell's ambassador to France, and Mammon as England's, in the middle of the
Hundred Years' War, is a gift: two diplomats of Hell, using the war and the plague as their
negotiating table.*

## The infernal government (Grimorium Verum / Grand Grimoire)

- **Lucifer**: Emperor. **Beelzebuth**: Prince. **Astaroth**: Grand Duke.
- **Lucifuge Rofocale**: Prime Minister; controls the wealth of the world. The one a sorcerer
  actually bargains with.
- **Satanachia**: Grand General; power over women.
- **Agaliarept**: General; reveals secrets of courts and councils.
- **Fleurety**: Lieutenant-General; works by night, raises hail.
- **Sargatanas**: Brigadier; grants invisibility and opens locks.
- **Nebiros**: Field Marshal and inspector-general; knows the virtues of metals, plants and
  animals, and teaches necromancy. 🜏

## The Watchers (1 Enoch)

Angels who descended on Mount Hermon, took human wives, fathered the giants (Nephilim) and taught
forbidden arts. Enoch lists 200, led by twenty chiefs. Key names and what they taught:

- **Shemihazah (Semjaza)**: leader; bound the others by a mutual oath.
- **Azazel**: taught metalworking, weapons, armour and cosmetics; bound hand and foot in the
  desert of **Dudael** until the Judgment. Also the goat "for Azazel" of Leviticus 16.
- **Armaros**: the resolving of enchantments. **Baraqiel**: astrology.
  **Kokabiel**: the constellations. **Shamsiel**: the signs of the sun.
  **Sariel**: the course of the moon. **Penemue**: writing with ink and paper.
  **Gadreel**: led Eve astray; taught the weapons of war.
- The **Nephilim**, their giant children, whose spirits became the evil spirits after the
  Flood (1 Enoch 15).

*Watchers fit fallen scholars: plague-physicians and astrologers who blamed the conjunction of
1345 were, knowingly or not, reading a Watcher's lessons.*

## Scriptural and apocryphal figures

- **Satan**: "the adversary"; the accuser of Job (Job 1–2), the tempter (Matthew 4).
- **Lucifer**: "How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning!" (Isaiah 14:12).
- **Beelzebub / Baal-zebub**: the god of Ekron (2 Kings 1); "prince of the devils"
  (Matthew 12:24). Lord of the Flies. 🜏
- **Belial**: "worthlessness"; "what concord hath Christ with Belial?" (2 Corinthians 6:15).
- **Abaddon / Apollyon**: "the Destroyer", angel of the bottomless pit and king of the
  locusts (Revelation 9:11). 🜏
- **Asmodeus**: killed the seven husbands of Sarah on their wedding nights; driven off by the
  smoke of a fish's heart and liver, and bound in Upper Egypt by **Raphael** (Tobit 3, 6, 8).
- **Legion**: "My name is Legion: for we are many"; driven into the Gadarene swine (Mark 5:9).
- **Leviathan**: the sea-dragon (Job 41; Isaiah 27:1, "the crooked serpent").
- **Behemoth**: the great land-beast (Job 40).
- **Mammon**: riches personified (Matthew 6:24).
- **Moloch**: the god to whom children were passed through the fire (Leviticus 18:21). 🜏
  *A plague of children.*
- **Mastema**: chief of the evil spirits in Jubilees; God leaves him a tenth of them to test
  mankind.
- **Lilith**: the night creature of Isaiah 34:14; in later legend Adam's first wife and a
  killer of infants.
- **The Dragon, the Beast and the False Prophet** (Revelation 12–13, 16:13).
- **The Four Horsemen** (Revelation 6): the white rider (conquest), the red (war), the black
  (famine) and the **pale rider, Death, "and Hell followed with him"**, given power "to kill with
  sword, and with hunger, and with death, and with the beasts of the earth." 🜏 *Already
  foreshadowed in Chapter I.*

| Ghagiel | the hinderers | Beelzebub |
| Satariel | the concealers | Lucifuge |
| Gamchicoth | the disturbers | Astaroth |
| Golachab | the burners | Asmodeus |

| A'arab Zaraq | the ravens of dispersion | Baal |

## Angels

### The nine choirs (Pseudo-Dionysius)
1. **Seraphim**: the burning ones, six-winged, around the throne (Isaiah 6).
2. **Cherubim**: guardians of Eden's gate with the flaming sword (Genesis 3:24); four-faced (Ezekiel 1).
3. **Thrones**: the wheels within wheels, full of eyes (Ezekiel 1, 10).
4. **Dominions**, 5. **Virtues** (worker of miracles), 6. **Powers** (warriors against the
   fallen).
7. **Principalities**: guardians of nations and cities (the "prince of Persia" of Daniel 10).
8. **Archangels**. 9. **Angels**, including guardian angels.

*Michaëlis' demons list the choir they fell from. A fallen Throne should feel like a broken
Throne.*

### The seven archangels (1 Enoch 20)
- **Michael**: prince of the heavenly host; wars with the dragon (Revelation 12); weighs souls
  at the Judgment. 🜏 *France's own: Mont-Saint-Michel.*
- **Gabriel**: messenger (Daniel 8–9; Luke 1).
- **Raphael**: "God heals"; guide of Tobias; **binder of Asmodeus**; patron of physicians and
  travellers. 🜏 *The most natural heavenly ally against the plague.*
- **Uriel**: "light of God"; watcher over the world and Tartarus; guide of Enoch and Ezra.
- **Raguel**: "friend of God"; brings vengeance on the world of lights.
- **Sariel** (Saraqael): set over the spirits who sin.
- **Remiel** (Jeremiel): set over those who rise from the dead.

### Angels of plague and judgment 🜏
- **The destroying angel** (Exodus 12:23): passed over the doors marked with blood. *The red
  crosses on the doors of Bordeaux.*
- **The angel of the pestilence** (2 Samuel 24): sent against Jerusalem after David's census;
  **seventy thousand died**. He stayed his hand at the threshing floor of Araunah when David
  built an altar. *The biblical model for a plague that can be stopped by the right sacrifice
  in the right place.*
- **The seven angels with the seven bowls of wrath** (Revelation 16): the first pours out "a
  noisome and grievous sore" on those who bear the mark of the beast.
- **The seven angels with the seven trumpets** (Revelation 8–9): the fifth trumpet opens the
  pit, and Abaddon's locusts come out.
- **Samael**: in Talmudic lore the angel of death, the accuser and seducer. It's ambiguous
  whether he is fallen: a great "is he an angel or a devil?" figure.
- **Azrael**: the angel of death in Islamic tradition (known to medieval Christians through
  translation). Could reach France by way of Spain.

### Other
- **Metatron**: the heavenly scribe, once the man Enoch (3 Enoch).
- **Sandalphon**: weaves the prayers of the faithful into garlands for God.
- **Guardian angels**: every soul has one (Matthew 18:10). *Does a plague-dead child's guardian
  grieve? What does it do next?*

## Plague saints (not angels, but the other side of the ledger)

- **St Sebastian**: arrows as plague darts; the premier plague saint of the 14th century.
- **St Roch**: pilgrim healer with his dog and a bubo on his thigh (venerated slightly after
  1348; we can move him).
- **St Anthony the Great**: patron against "St Anthony's fire" (ergotism); the Antonine
  hospitallers.
- **St Rosalia**, **St Charles Borromeo**: later plague saints (Palermo 1624; Milan 1576).
- **The Virgin of Mercy** (*Mater Misericordiae*): shelters the faithful under her cloak from
  God's plague arrows.
